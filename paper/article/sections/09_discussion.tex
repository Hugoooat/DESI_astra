\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{What is robust.} The comparison was made with three derivative estimators (emulator, regression over pairs, Quijote finite differences), two covariances, four fiducial HODs, two tracer populations (LRG-like galaxies, halos) and several data vectors. Two conclusions never changed: ASTRA split and density split carry comparable information (error ratio 0.75--1.2 for the same data vector, with four or six parameters, in the DESI-like analysis with the independent covariance, 0.6--2 in the reliable Quijote forecasts), and they are complementary, their combination gaining 23\% to a factor 2.4. The ranking for individual parameters changed with the analysis choices (Table~\ref{tab:evolution}), at the 10--40\% level, which is the size of the effect of those choices; we therefore do not claim a winner. Where a trend is consistent, density split is slightly ahead on $\omega_b$, $\Omega_m$ and $n_s$ with the random-quartile cross-correlations, and ASTRA split ahead on $\sigma_8$ through its environment auto-correlations and tracer environments (Quijote). In particular, the advantage of ASTRA split on $\sigma_8$ found with the single-box covariance disappears with the independent covariance.

\paragraph{What we learnt about the methodology.} (i) Emulator suites in which the HOD is drawn independently in each cosmology do not allow naive finite differences: the HOD mismatch dominates the cosmological response by an order of magnitude, and paired differences do not cancel the galaxy sampling noise of different HOD catalogues. (ii) Single-box covariances, even with many subvolumes and lines of sight, can bias the \emph{comparison} of statistics, not only their absolute errors. (iii) Environment statistics of sparse tracers have noisy derivatives, which a six-parameter Fisher matrix amplifies along its degenerate directions; comparing two Fisher estimators that bracket the truth identifies the reliable forecasts. (iv) The clustering statistics respond essentially to a single combination of the 12 HOD parameters.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i) The forecasts are for one $(500\Mpch)^3$ volume, where the errors are comparable to the prior widths: they compare information content and are not realistic constraints. (ii) The inference is calibrated but not yet discriminating: at this volume the prior dominates, and for a full box the emulator error does. (iii) The Quijote halos are ten times sparser than the LRGs, and ASTRA's adaptive scale is correspondingly larger. (iv) The emulator suite has a single HOD realisation per point, whose galaxy sampling noise sets the floor of the emulator error.

\paragraph{Improvements.} A learning curve shows that the emulator error has reached a plateau at about half the statistical error of a $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc box: more training catalogues of the same kind would barely help. Several HOD realisations (seeds) per point, or HOD-matched catalogues generated with AbacusHOD in all cosmologies, are needed both to lower this floor, which would make an inference on a DESI-like volume discriminating, and to obtain clean derivatives in the DESI-like setting. Larger sets of independent boxes would remove the remaining dependence on the covariance.

\paragraph{Beyond quartiles.} Being local and free of smoothing, ASTRA provides more than a density ranking: a classification of every galaxy into voids, sheets, filaments and knots, a continuous environment value usable as a mark, and a natural treatment of survey masks through the random points. The first test of the classes on Quijote (Section~\ref{sec:classes}) is the most promising result of this work for ASTRA: despite their imbalance, the classes with their auto-correlations constrain $\sigma_8$ two to three times better than density split, robustly across compressions. Natural next steps are the probabilistic classes of \cite{ASTRA} (classification repeated over many random catalogues, which gives every point a probability of belonging to each class and could make the void and knot classes usable), thresholds adapted to the density of the sample, and an emulator of class statistics for DESI.
