\section{Conclusions}
% =====================================================================

We measured how much cosmological information ASTRA environment statistics add to the galaxy two-point function, and how they compare with density split, on the same DESI-like mocks and Quijote halos, with dedicated covariance and derivative datasets, a neural-network emulator and an end-to-end inference test. The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gain over the two-point function.} Splitting galaxies and random points by ASTRA environment improves the constraints on $\omega_b$, $\omega_{\rm cdm}$, $n_s$, $\sigma_8$, $w_0$ and $w_a$ by factors 1.6--3.1 over the galaxy 2PCF, and by factors 2.3--8.5 when the environment auto-correlations are added.
\item \textbf{ASTRA and density split carry comparable information, and are complementary.} In the baseline analysis their errors differ by less than 17\% on every parameter, within 1.7 times the emulator uncertainty; across all the covariances, fiducial points and derivative estimators we tried, the ratio stays between 0.75 and 1.3. Combined, they improve on the better of the two by 23\% to a factor 2.4.
\item \textbf{They do not extract the same information.} In the voids (Q1) the two methods are equivalent. In the dense regions (Q4), ASTRA is more constraining on $n_s$ and $\sigma_8$ and density split on $\omega_b$ and $\omega_{\rm cdm}$; with the auto-correlations, the lead of density split on $\omega_b$ and $\omega_{\rm cdm}$ is largest in the voids, and the lead of ASTRA on $\sigma_8$ comes from the dense regions. With Quijote halos, ASTRA is more constraining on $\sigma_8$ by a factor 1.5--2.1 when its auto-correlations or tracer environments are used. The intermediate ASTRA quartile carries little information.
\item \textbf{What ASTRA measures.} The ASTRA and density-split environments agree on the broad picture (rank correlation 0.72) but differ point by point. The ASTRA value $r$ itself hardly responds to cosmology: the information lies in where the environments are and how they cluster. ASTRA adapts its scale to the local density, so it must be modelled at the density and random-to-galaxy ratio of the data.
\item \textbf{An emulator of environment statistics.} A neural-network emulator of the 33 statistics as functions of 8 cosmological and 12 HOD parameters reproduces held-out cosmologies to 0.4--0.9 times the error of a $(2\,h^{-1}{\rm Gpc})^3$ box. Trained also on catalogue differences, it gives derivatives at fixed HOD that agree with HOD-corrected pair differences to 1--13\%; an independent regression over the derivative grid gives the same ranking of the methods, but absolute errors that differ by factors 2--6. Its accuracy is limited by the galaxy sampling noise of single catalogues.
\item \textbf{An end-to-end inference test.} An MCMC with the emulator on held-out data recovers the parameters without bias (offsets $<0.4\sigma$) with the expected coverage, but one $(500\Mpch)^3$ volume is prior-dominated and a full box is limited by the emulator error; discriminating between statistics by inference requires several HOD realisations per training point.
\item \textbf{Method.} Emulator suites with an independent HOD per cosmology do not allow naive finite differences (the HOD term dominates the cosmological one by a factor 10--15); covariances from a single simulation box can bias the comparison of statistics; and the derivatives of environment statistics of sparse tracers are noisy, which comparing two bracketing Fisher estimators detects.
\end{itemize}
The original ASTRA cosmic-web classes reduce at these densities to sheets and filaments, which is why we used quartiles; tested on Quijote, they nevertheless constrain $\sigma_8$ two to three times better than density split, a lead for future work together with an emulator of class statistics for DESI.
