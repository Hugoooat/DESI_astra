\title{Environment-dependent galaxy clustering from ASTRA tessellations:\\
a comparison with density-split clustering}
\author{Hugo Nguyen}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Density-split clustering, the clustering of regions ranked by their smoothed local density, recovers part of the non-Gaussian cosmological information of the galaxy field. We compare it with ASTRA split, in which the environment of a point is defined without smoothing, from its neighbours in the Delaunay tessellation of the galaxies and of a random catalogue. Both are measured on the same DESI-like luminous red galaxy mocks at $z=0.5$ (AbacusSummit), with the same random points and estimators. Cosmological derivatives are obtained from a neural-network emulator of the statistics as a function of cosmology and galaxy--halo connection (HOD), and the covariance from 1786 independent boxes of the official DESI mocks; a second analysis with Quijote halos provides clean finite-difference derivatives. The two methods carry comparable information: the ratio of their marginalised errors on $\omega_b$, $\omega_{\rm cdm}$, $n_s$ and $\sigma_8$ lies between 0.75 and 1.1 in the DESI-like analysis, density split being slightly ahead on most parameters. They are complementary: their combination improves on the better of the two by 25\% ($\sigma_8$) to a factor 2.4 ($\omega_b$). With Quijote halos, ASTRA split is more constraining on $\sigma_8$ by a factor 1.5--2 when its environment auto-correlations or tracer environments are used. ASTRA is an adaptive-scale statistic, much more sensitive than density split to the sampling density. We document the methodological pitfalls met along the way and how we addressed them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Galaxy clustering constrains the expansion history, the growth of structure, the matter content and the properties of dark energy and neutrinos. Spectroscopic surveys such as DESI now map galaxies in volumes where the statistical errors are small enough that the information beyond the two-point function becomes worth extracting. Late-time structure is strongly non-Gaussian, and two-point summaries miss part of its information.

Among the statistics proposed to recover it, density-split clustering \cite{Paillas2023, Paillas2024} ranks random points by the smoothed galaxy density around them, splits them into quantiles, and measures the auto-correlation of each quantile and its cross-correlation with the galaxies. Combined with a simulation-based emulator \cite{CuestaLazaro2024}, it improves $\Lambda$CDM constraints by factors of several with respect to the two-point function.

ASTRA \cite{ASTRA} classifies the cosmic web from the Delaunay tessellation of the galaxies together with a uniform random catalogue: the environment of a point is set by the fraction of galaxies among its neighbours, i.e.\ at an adaptive, local scale and without smoothing. We ask whether environment quartiles defined with ASTRA carry cosmological information beyond the galaxy two-point function, and how they compare with density split when both are measured on the same mocks, with the same random points and the same estimators.

We use three sets of simulations, each for what the others cannot provide: the AbacusSummit emulator suite of DESI-like mocks (measurements and emulator), the official DESI AbacusHOD mocks (an independent covariance) and the Quijote simulations (clean finite-difference derivatives). Estimating derivatives and covariances turned out to be the main difficulty; the successive approaches, the choices made and how the results evolved are summarised in Section~\ref{sec:choices} and detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:path}. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} explains how to reproduce the paper, and Appendix~\ref{app:maths} gives the mathematical details.

\section{Simulations}
\label{sec:sims}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{AbacusSummit emulator suite.} DESI emulator catalogues of luminous red galaxies (LRG) at $z=0.5$, built on AbacusSummit \cite{Maksimova2021} boxes of $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc with the AbacusHOD model \cite{Yuan2022} (12 HOD parameters sampled in the prior of \cite{Yuan2023}); every catalogue contains $4\times10^6$ galaxies ($\bar n = 5\times10^{-4}\,h^3{\rm Mpc}^{-3}$). The suite covers 85 cosmologies, each with a single phase and 500 HOD draws. Two properties drive our methodology: the 500 files of a cosmology are \emph{different HOD models} (a Latin hypercube), not realisations of one model, and the HOD behind a given file index differs from one cosmology to another. \section{Environment estimators}
\label{sec:env}
% =====================================================================

Both estimators are evaluated on the full periodic box and on the same uniform random points ($N_r = N_g$, fixed seed).

\paragraph{ASTRA split.} Galaxies and random points are Delaunay-triangulated together. For every point, with $N_{\rm data}$ and $N_{\rm rand}$ the numbers of galaxies and randoms among its Delaunay neighbours,
\begin{equation}
r = \frac{N_{\rm data}-N_{\rm rand}}{N_{\rm data}+N_{\rm rand}} \in [-1,1]
\label{eq:astra_r}
\end{equation}
is close to 1 in dense regions and to $-1$ in empty ones. The tessellation of each $(500\Mpch)^3$ subvolume includes a periodic padding of $30\Mpch$, which removes edge effects.

\paragraph{Density split.} The reference method \cite{Paillas2023}: galaxies are painted on a $5\Mpch$ mesh (CIC), the field is smoothed with a Gaussian of radius $R_s=10\Mpch$, and the density contrast $\delta_R$ is read at every point.

\paragraph{Quartiles.} For each method, points are ranked by $r$ or $\delta_R$ and split into four equal-size quartiles, Q1 (most underdense) to Q4 (most overdense), ties of the discrete $r$ being broken at random. We use two populations, ranked separately: the \emph{random quartiles} (random points, the query points of density split, sampling environments by volume) and the \emph{galaxy quartiles} (galaxies, sampling environments by mass).

\paragraph{Comparison.} The two estimators are strongly but not perfectly correlated (Figure~\ref{fig:env}): the Spearman correlation between $r$ and $\delta_R$ is $0.72$; 63\% of the ASTRA Q1 (Q4) random points are also in the density-split Q1 (Q4), while the intermediate quartiles are largely mixed. ASTRA identifies the same structures but redistributes many points, as expected from an adaptive rather than a fixed scale (Figure~\ref{fig:map}).

\paragraph{Response to cosmology.} Figure~\ref{fig:response} shows how the mean environment of each quartile of the Quijote halos shifts when a cosmological parameter is varied, in units of its scatter between realisations. The density-split contrast $\delta_R$ responds strongly (up to $7\sigma$ for $\sigma_8$), and antisymmetrically for the $\pm$ variations; the ASTRA value $r$ hardly moves ($<1\sigma$). This is expected: $r$ is normalised locally by the random points and is nearly insensitive to the overall amplitude of the field. The cosmological information of ASTRA split therefore lies in the clustering of its environments rather than in the values of $r$.

\section{Clustering measurements}
\label{sec:clustering}
% =====================================================================

Each $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc box is cut into 64 subvolumes of $500\Mpch$, each treated as an independent survey with open boundaries: correlation functions are measured with the Landy--Szalay estimator \cite{LandySzalay1993} and geometry randoms, with \texttt{pycorr}/\texttt{Corrfunc} \cite{Sinha2020}, along a fixed line of sight, and projected on the multipoles $\ell=0,2$. For each method, population and quartile we measure the quartile--galaxy cross-correlation and the quartile auto-correlation, together with the galaxy two-point correlation function (2PCF).

The two methods produce qualitatively identical signals (Figure~\ref{fig:multipoles}): negative Q1--galaxy cross-correlations (the profile of galaxies around underdense regions), positive Q4 ones above the 2PCF, enhanced auto-correlations of the extreme quartiles, and quadrupoles of opposite signs around under- and overdense regions (outflow and infall). The ASTRA signals are weaker, by a factor $\sim1.6$ at the peak of the Q1 and Q4 cross-correlations, consistent with the mixing of the quartiles: ASTRA relies on $\sim15$ neighbours per point and dilutes the contrast between environments. Whether this costs information is a question about signal \emph{and} noise, addressed with the forecasts. Real-space measurements and the galaxy quartiles are shown in Appendix~\ref{app:path}.

\section{Forecasting framework}
\label{sec:framework}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Data vectors.} By default, the cross-correlations of the extreme quartiles Q1 and Q4 with the galaxies, $\ell=0,2$, $20\le s\le150\Mpch$ in $10\Mpch$ bins (52 numbers per method), with or without the 2PCF; variants add the quartile auto-correlations or use the galaxy quartiles.

\paragraph{Covariance.} Measured on the 1786 official small boxes, i.e.\ 1786 independent $(500\Mpch)^3$ volumes treated exactly like our subvolumes. Forecasts are quoted for this volume of $0.125\,(h^{-1}{\rm Gpc})^3$. The correlation matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:corr}) shows strong correlations between neighbouring scales, between the Q4 cross-correlations and the 2PCF, and anti-correlations between the Q1 and Q4 blocks. The ASTRA and density-split blocks of the same quartile are very strongly correlated: being measured on the same galaxies, their noise is largely common. This is the origin of their complementarity: when two measurements share most of their noise but respond differently to the parameters, their combination cancels part of that noise and gains much more than independent measurements would.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{v2_corr.png}
\caption{Correlation matrix of the 2PCF and of the Q1, Q4 cross-correlations of ASTRA split and density split (1786 official small boxes).}
\label{fig:corr}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Derivatives: an emulator.} Because the HOD differs between the cosmologies of the emulator suite, finite differences between them measure mostly the HOD change, not the cosmological one (Section~\ref{sec:choices}). We therefore model each statistic as a function of 8 cosmological ($\omega_b$, $\omega_{\rm cdm}$, $\sigma_8$, $n_s$, $\alpha_s$, $N_{\rm ur}$, $w_0$, $w_a$) and 12 HOD parameters with a neural network, as in SUNBIRD \cite{CuestaLazaro2024}, and take its derivatives at exactly fixed HOD by automatic differentiation. Each statistic has an ensemble of 10 networks (three hidden layers of 256 SiLU units) trained on 999 catalogues of the 85 cosmologies, densified around the fiducial point, with a loss that includes the differences between catalogues of the derivative grid (Appendix~\ref{app:maths}). On 8 cosmologies never seen in training, the emulator reproduces all the statistics to 0.4--0.9 times the statistical error of a $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc box, i.e.\ about ten times below the error of the volume of the forecasts (Figure~\ref{fig:emu}); it also predicts the differences between held-out catalogues of the derivative grid at the noise level of these differences. The emulator error is added to the covariance. Figure~\ref{fig:derivs} shows the derivatives in units of the statistical error: the two methods respond very similarly to $\omega_{\rm cdm}$ and $n_s$, mostly through the monopole at $s<75\Mpch$; the Q1 (void) cross-correlations respond with the opposite sign to Q4 and to the 2PCF, which is what breaks degeneracies; $\sigma_8$ acts mostly on the quadrupoles at fixed HOD.

\begin{figure*}[t]

The uncertainty due to the emulator training is estimated from the scatter of the forecasts of the individual networks.

\section{Analysis choices and how the results evolved}
\label{sec:choices}
% =====================================================================

The framework above is the result of successive corrections, each motivated by a failure of the previous approach (Table~\ref{tab:evolution}; details and figures in Appendix~\ref{app:path}). We report the intermediate results because they show which conclusions depend on the analysis choices.

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{p{4.2cm}p{6.3cm}p{6.3cm}}
\toprule
Change & Problem solved & Effect on ASTRA vs DS \\
\midrule
Averaged HOD files $\to$ HOD-matched pairs & the 500 files are different HOD models: their scatter ($\sim55\%$) swamped the cosmology ($\sim1\%$) & derivatives were pure noise \\
Finite differences $\to$ emulator & the residual HOD mismatch of the pairs dominated the derivatives by a factor $\sim10$; a calibrated HOD correction (8\% accurate) was not enough & Fisher matrices became well defined \\
Emulator $\to$ emulator trained on catalogue differences & the derivatives of individual networks scattered by 70--170\%; the ranking changed between trainings & ranking stable between trainings; cross-checked by an independent regression over the derivative grid \\
192 subvolumes of one box $\to$ 1786 independent boxes & covariance from a single box, three correlated lines of sight & ratio $\sigma_{\rm DS}/\sigma_{\rm ASTRA}$ on $\sigma_8$: $1.12\to0.85$--$1.11$; ASTRA's apparent lead disappears \\
Quijote, 6 parameters $\to$ reliable subsets & derivative noise dominates the degenerate directions ($\Omega_b$, $h$, $M_\nu$) & ranking stable only for $(\Omega_m,\sigma_8)$, $(\Omega_m,n_s,\sigma_8)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The main changes of the analysis, the problem each solved, and its effect on the comparison.}
\label{tab:evolution}
\end{table*}

The baseline analysis was fixed after these steps: for the DESI-like mocks, the emulator trained on catalogue differences, the covariance of the official small boxes and the official HOD as fiducial point; for Quijote, the parameter subsets whose forecasts do not depend on the derivative noise. Other choices are treated as robustness tests.

\subsection{DESI-like mocks}

Figure~\ref{fig:ratios} and Table~\ref{tab:sigma} give the baseline forecasts. Both methods improve on the 2PCF by factors 1.7--2.7 depending on the parameter. Between them, the ratio of the errors $\sigma_{\rm DS}/\sigma_{\rm ASTRA}$ for the random-quartile cross-correlations is 0.86, 1.01, 0.86 and 0.85 for $\omega_b$, $\omega_{\rm cdm}$, $n_s$ and $\sigma_8$: density split is slightly ahead, by $\sim15\%$, except on $\omega_{\rm cdm}$. The two are clearly complementary: combined, they improve on the better one by 25\% ($\sigma_8$) to a factor 2.4 ($\omega_b$), and the combination with the 2PCF is the most constraining. The variants (auto-correlations, galaxy quartiles, single environments) give ratios between 0.5 and 1.4 with no consistent winner (Figure~\ref{fig:ratios}).

\subsection{Quijote halos}

With Quijote the derivatives are clean of HOD effects but noisy: environment statistics of sparse halos are noisy, and the noise of the average of 500 derivative pairs is comparable to the information along the degenerate directions of the six-parameter space. We identify the forecasts that do not depend on this noise by comparing two estimators that bracket the true Fisher information \cite{CoultonWandelt2023}, and keep those where they agree within a factor 1.5 (Appendix~\ref{app:maths}). Only the subsets $(\Omega_m,\sigma_8)$ and $(\Omega_m,n_s,\sigma_8)$ pass this test, for all compressions of the data vector (Figure~\ref{fig:qsubsets}); $\Omega_b$, $h$ and $M_\nu$ cannot be constrained reliably with 500 pairs.

The reliable forecasts (Table~\ref{tab:quijote}) agree with the DESI-like analysis for the random-quartile cross-correlations: density split is as constraining as ASTRA split or more ($\sigma_{\rm DS}/\sigma_{\rm ASTRA}=0.6$--$1.1$). When the quartile auto-correlations are added, or with the halo quartiles, ASTRA split is more constraining on $\sigma_8$ by a factor 1.5--2: its information lies more in the clustering of its environments with themselves and in the environments of the tracers than in the tracer profiles around random environments.

\subsection{Inference with the emulator}

We sampled the 20-parameter posterior with the emulator (\texttt{emcee}, uniform priors) on data it has not seen: subvolumes of 2 catalogues of each of the 8 test cosmologies and 8 subvolumes of the official base box (120 chains). The posteriors are unbiased and calibrated or slightly conservative (the truth is within $1\sigma$ in 70--100\% of the runs). They are however barely narrower than the priors (Figure~\ref{fig:mcmc}): one $(500\Mpch)^3$ volume with 12 free HOD parameters constrains only $\omega_{\rm cdm}$ and $\sigma_8$, by 10--15\%, and the statistics differ by a few per cent. For a full $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc box the limitation is the opposite: the emulator error equals the statistical error. Distinguishing the statistics through inference requires both a large volume and an emulator error well below the statistical error of that volume (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Sensitivity to the sampling density}
\label{sec:robustness}
% =====================================================================

The padding of the tessellation is converged (doubling it changes nothing), but ASTRA is very sensitive to the sampling (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness}). Doubling the number of random points shifts the ASTRA cross-correlations by up to $4\sigma$ (density split: $<0.2\sigma$), and thinning the galaxies by 2 (5) by 7--10 (20--35)$\sigma$ (density split: $\sim1$ (3--5)$\sigma$); the $r$ values of the same galaxies in the full and in a thinned-by-2 sample have a rank correlation of only 0.65. This is intrinsic: ASTRA is an \emph{adaptive-scale} statistic whose effective scale is the distance between neighbours, $\propto\bar n^{-1/3}$, so it changes with the density, mostly below $50\Mpch$. It is not a limitation as long as ASTRA is modelled at the density and random-to-galaxy ratio of the data, as in this work, but comparisons must be made at equal density.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{What is robust.} Across derivative estimators, covariances, fiducial HODs, tracers and data vectors, two conclusions never changed: ASTRA split and density split carry comparable information, and they are complementary. The ranking for individual parameters changed with the analysis choices (Table~\ref{tab:evolution}), at the 10--40\% level, which is the size of the effect of those choices; we therefore do not claim a winner. Where a trend is consistent, density split is slightly ahead on $\omega_b$, $\Omega_m$ and $n_s$ with the random-quartile cross-correlations, and ASTRA split ahead on $\sigma_8$ through its environment auto-correlations and tracer environments (Quijote).

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i) The forecasts are for one $(500\Mpch)^3$ volume, where the errors are comparable to the prior widths: they compare information content and are not realistic constraints. (ii) The inference is calibrated but not yet discriminating: at this volume the prior dominates, and for a full box the emulator error does. (iii) The Quijote halos are ten times sparser than the LRGs, and ASTRA's adaptive scale is correspondingly larger. (iv) The emulator suite has a single HOD realisation per point, whose galaxy sampling noise limits the precision of the cosmological response learnt by the emulator.

\paragraph{Improvements.} A learning curve shows that the emulator error has reached a plateau at about half the statistical error of a $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc box, set by the sampling noise of single HOD catalogues (Appendix~\ref{app:path}): more training catalogues would barely help. Several HOD realisations (seeds) per point, or HOD-matched catalogues generated with AbacusHOD, are needed both to lower this floor, which would make an inference on a DESI-like volume discriminating, and to obtain clean derivatives in the DESI-like setting.

\paragraph{Beyond quartiles.} Being local and free of smoothing, ASTRA provides more than a density ranking: a classification of every galaxy into voids, sheets, filaments and knots, a continuous environment value usable as a mark, and a natural treatment of survey masks through the random points. Its quartile auto-correlations already appear to break degeneracies (Quijote); exploiting these other outputs is left to future work.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
% =====================================================================

We compared ASTRA split, an environment estimator built on the Delaunay tessellation of galaxies and random points, with standard density split, on the same DESI-like mocks and Quijote halos.
\begin{itemize}
\item The two classifications are strongly but not perfectly correlated ($\rho\simeq0.72$); ASTRA produces weaker environment contrasts.
\item Both improve on the 2PCF by factors $\sim2$. They carry comparable information (error ratio 0.75--1.1 in the DESI-like baseline, density split slightly ahead) and are complementary (25\% to a factor 2.4 gain when combined). With Quijote halos, ASTRA is more constraining on $\sigma_8$ through its environment auto-correlations and tracer environments.
\item ASTRA is an adaptive-scale statistic, very sensitive to the sampling density, and must be modelled at the density of the data.
\item Emulator suites with independent HOD draws per cosmology do not allow naive finite differences, single-box covariances bias the comparison of statistics, and environment statistics of sparse tracers have noisy derivatives; we showed how each of these was diagnosed and addressed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical details}
\label{app:maths}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Fisher matrix and Hartlap factor.} For a data vector $\boldsymbol d$ with covariance $C$ estimated from $N_s$ samples, $F_{ij} = \partial_i\boldsymbol d^T \hat C^{-1}\partial_j\boldsymbol d$ with $\hat C^{-1} = \frac{N_s-N_d-2}{N_s-1}C^{-1}$ \cite{Hartlap2007}; marginalised errors are $\sigma_i = \sqrt{(F^{-1})_{ii}}$. HOD parameters are marginalised by adding a Gaussian prior $P_h$ (width 0.29 times the prior range, the standard deviation of the uniform prior) to the HOD block and inverting the full $16\times16$ matrix.

\paragraph{Compression.} Each data vector is compressed to $\boldsymbol t = W^T\boldsymbol d$, $W=\tilde C^{-1}D$ ($D$: derivatives with respect to the 16 parameters, $\tilde C$: Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage covariance), and the $16\times16$ covariance of $\boldsymbol t$ is estimated from the samples. The compression is lossless if $\tilde C=C$, and otherwise loses information without biasing the forecast; it keeps the Hartlap factor close to unity for long data vectors.

\paragraph{Noise of the derivatives.} Derivatives estimated as an average over $N$ pairs are $\hat D = D + \epsilon$, with a noise of covariance $\Sigma_D/N$. The standard Fisher estimator then has the expectation
\begin{equation}
\langle \hat D^T C^{-1}\hat D\rangle = D^T C^{-1} D + \mathrm{Tr}\big(C^{-1}\Sigma_D\big)/N .
\end{equation}
The second term is a positive bias: the noise adds spurious information. It is roughly the same in all directions of parameter space, but matters only where the true information $D^TC^{-1}D$ is small, i.e.\ in the degenerate directions; the marginalised errors, set by the inverse of the matrix, are controlled precisely by these weak directions. We use three tests:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the noise fraction $\mathrm{Tr}(C^{-1}\Sigma_D)/(N F_{ii})$; above 20--30\% the forecast is suspect;
\item the bracketing of \cite{CoultonWandelt2023}: the standard estimator is biased high; a compressed estimator, in which half of the simulations define the compression and the other half measure its information, is biased low. If both give the same error the noise does not matter; if they differ by factors 2--10 the result reflects the noise, not the physics. We call a forecast reliable when they agree within a factor 1.5, and quote their geometric mean, which is nearly unbiased;
\item convergence: the forecast is recomputed with $N/2$ pairs.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Volume.} The forecasts are computed for one $(500\Mpch)^3$ volume, where the absolute errors are comparable to the prior widths; this is the nature of a comparison of information content and is not corrected. The ratios $\sigma_{\rm DS}/\sigma_{\rm ASTRA}$ do not depend on the volume as long as the covariance scales as $1/V$ and the emulator error is negligible, which holds at this volume.

\paragraph{Emulator loss.} With outputs standardised by the statistical error of a $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc box $\sigma_{\rm box}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L = \Big\langle \frac{(f(x_i) - y_i)^2}{\sigma_{\rm box}^2}\Big\rangle + \lambda \Big\langle \frac{[(f(x_i) - f(x_j)) - (y_i - y_j)]^2}{\sigma_{ij}^2 + \epsilon^2}\Big\rangle,
\end{equation}
where the second average runs over 2544 pairs of catalogues of the derivative grid near the fiducial HOD and of the same cosmology, $\sigma_{ij}$ is the error of their difference (64 paired subvolumes), $\lambda=1$, $\epsilon=0.05\,\sigma_{\rm box}$. The difference term constrains how the statistics change between nearby points of parameter space, which is what the derivatives require.

\paragraph{Regression over pairs.} An emulator-independent estimate of the derivatives: for a pair $c_\pm$, all catalogues of each cosmology near the fiducial HOD $h_0$ are fitted with $\boldsymbol d_c(h) = \boldsymbol a_c + G\,V(h-h_0)$, with a response $G$ shared by the two cosmologies and $V$ the 3 most important directions of the HOD space (singular vectors of the HOD response measured on 72 fiducial catalogues); the derivative is $(\boldsymbol a_+ - \boldsymbol a_-)/(\theta_+-\theta_-)$, the difference at exactly $h_0$.

\paragraph{Inference.} Gaussian likelihood with the official covariance (rescaled to the data volume) plus the emulator error, uniform priors over the training range, \texttt{emcee} with 64 walkers and 8000 steps. Calibration is tested by the fraction of runs whose truth lies within $1\sigma$ of the posterior mean (68\% expected).

\section{Methodological path and intermediate results}
\label{app:path}
% =====================================================================

This appendix details the steps of Table~\ref{tab:evolution}.

\paragraph{1. HOD files are not realisations.} A first version averaged the statistics of 64 HOD files per cosmology to build covariances and derivatives. The files are different HOD models: their scatter ($\sim55\%$ on the monopoles) swamped the cosmological signal ($\sim1\%$), and the derivatives were compatible with noise (median $|S/N|<1$). That version also used periodic pair counts on a non-periodic cut box, a mid-point line of sight that erased the quadrupole, quartiles computed jointly on galaxies and randoms, a non-periodic tessellation, and ASTRA run on randoms alone, which made the random-quartile correlations vanish.

\paragraph{2. HOD-matched pairs.} We selected, for each $\pm$ pair, the two catalogues with the closest HODs (twice closer than random pairs) and differenced them subvolume by subvolume. The differences are precise once averaged over 192 samples, but are dominated by the residual HOD mismatch: a 2\% change of $\omega_b$ implied tens of per cent changes of the 2PCF, and the derivative vectors for $\omega_b$, $n_s$, $\sigma_8$ were 95--99\% correlated, making the Fisher matrices singular. Each HOD catalogue draws its own galaxies, so the difference of two catalogues with different HODs has an error comparable to that of a single box (1.05 times): pairing cancels cosmic variance but not galaxy sampling noise.

\paragraph{3. HOD calibration.} The HOD response measured on 72 fiducial catalogues and subtracted from the pairs was predicted with a 45\% error by a linear model and 8\% by a Gaussian process; with an HOD term ten times the signal, even 8\% leaves a residual of the size of the signal.

\paragraph{4. Emulator.} The first emulator reproduced held-out cosmologies to a fraction of the statistical error and agreed with the HOD-corrected pairs to 1--13\%, but trained without the derivative grid it did not capture the response to $\omega_{\rm cdm}$ below $70\Mpch$ (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}), whose effect at fixed $\theta_*$ and $\sigma_8$ is smaller than the emulator error.

\paragraph{5--7. Densification, regression, differences.} We added 16 HOD draws close to the fiducial in each cosmology of the grid (240 catalogues). This alone did not stabilise the derivatives (70--170\% scatter between networks). Training on catalogue differences halved the error of the predicted differences between held-out catalogues for $\omega_b$ and $\sigma_8$ (21\%$\to$10\%, 13\%$\to$7\%), down to their noise level; an extra small-scale weight gave a marginal gain but destabilised the auto-correlation forecasts and was dropped. The regression over pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:maths}) revealed that the clustering statistics respond essentially to a single combination of the 12 HOD parameters (singular values $1, 0.054, 0.014,\dots$), plausibly the galaxy bias.

\paragraph{Learning curve.} To decide whether more training catalogues would reduce the emulator error, we retrained the emulator of the 2PCF and of the Q1, Q4 random-quartile cross-correlations on 25, 50, 75 and 100\% of the training set. The median test error, in units of $\sigma_{\rm box}$, drops from 0.7--1.3 to 0.5--0.8 between 25 and 50\%, but only to 0.5--0.7 at 100\% (ASTRA Q1: 1.01, 0.59, 0.55, 0.51; density split Q4: 0.69, 0.51, 0.48, 0.46). The error has reached a plateau at $\simeq0.5\,\sigma_{\rm box}$, comparable to the sampling noise of a single catalogue: doubling the training set (about 230 node-hours) would improve it by $\sim10\%$ at most, and we did not do it. Reducing it further requires several HOD realisations per point rather than more points.

\paragraph{8. Covariance.} With the 192 subvolumes of one box (three correlated lines of sight), the emulator and the regression both gave ASTRA slightly ahead (ratios 1.0--1.3 on $\omega_b$, $n_s$, $\sigma_8$). With the 1786 independent boxes and the same derivatives, this lead disappeared (Table~\ref{tab:covbox}): the single-box covariance misrepresented the relative noise of the two methods.

\paragraph{9. Quijote.} The covariance is converged with 4000 realisations (errors change by $<1\%$ between 2000 and 4000 at fixed derivatives). An automatic convergence test first reported the opposite, because it compared full forecasts in which the derivative noise dominates; the extra processing it triggered was cancelled. About half of the fiducial catalogues of our copy were empty (incomplete download), without consequence given the convergence; all derivative sets are complete. The $\pm$ pairing works (differences of same-index realisations scatter 3--4 times less than of different indices). The derivatives remain noisy because Quijote is designed for matter and halo statistics (power spectrum, bispectrum), while our observables add two sources of noise the pairing cannot cancel: (1) sparse tracers, $\simeq50\,000$ halos per $(h^{-1}{\rm Gpc})^3$, i.e.\ $\simeq12\,000$ per quartile, with large shot noise; (2) the discrete classification, as a small change of cosmology moves halos and random points between quartiles in a way only partly common to the $+$ and $-$ simulations. This noise is amplified by the six-parameter Fisher matrix: $\Omega_b$, $h$, $n_s$ (and $M_\nu$) are strongly degenerate, their weakest combination is constrained by an information comparable to the noise of 500 pairs, and there the derivatives are noise-dominated (Appendix~\ref{app:maths}). Hence the restriction to $(\Omega_m,\sigma_8)$ and $(\Omega_m,n_s,\sigma_8)$, with $\Omega_b$, $h$, $n_s$ fixed by external data (BBN, CMB) as is common; $M_\nu$ would require denser tracers or more derivative simulations.

\paragraph{Figures of the first version not reproduced.} The first version included a ``systematic bias'' study, in which the real-space statistics were taken as the truth and the redshift-space ones as biased. Real-space environments are not observable, and here both the measurements and the emulator are in redshift space, so this comparison does not describe a bias of the analysis; the relevant systematic, the emulator error, is included in the covariance. The real/redshift-space comparison is kept as a diagnostic (Figure~\ref{fig:realz}).
